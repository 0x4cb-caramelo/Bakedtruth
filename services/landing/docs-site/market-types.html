Market types

In one bite: six kinds of question, each with its answer already written on-chain. If we can't read it, we don't list it.

Each market type has a reader: a piece of the program that opens one kind of Solana account, reads one number and answers YES or NO. No reader, no market type.

The six types

TypeWhat it asksExampleWho could push the result
PriceDoes a large asset's price touch a target before the end date?"Does SOL touch $300 by Dec 31?"Nobody. It's the whole market for that asset
BurnDoes a token's total supply fall to a number?"Does the supply of token T fall to 900M by the 30th?"Whoever holds the token and burns it. It costs them the tokens
Locked liquidityDoes a Meteora pool have at least X locked forever?"Does the T/SOL pool reach X locked liquidity by the 30th?"Whoever adds liquidity and locks it. The money stays locked
Stake pool sizeDoes a stake pool hold at least X SOL at the start of an epoch?"Does JitoSOL start epoch E with X SOL or more?"Whoever deposits. The money has to stay through the epoch change
Validator stakeDoes a validator have at least X SOL staked with it in an epoch?"Does validator V enter epoch E with X SOL or more?"Whoever stakes with it before the epoch changes
Launch graduationDoes a token's Meteora launch curve complete by the end date?"Does token T's Meteora curve complete by the 30th?"Anyone who pays the rest of the curve. The token's own team is best placed to do it

A few words, if you're new to Solana:

  • Supply is the total amount of a token. A burn destroys tokens forever.
  • Locked liquidity is money put into a trading pool and locked there for good. Meteora is one of Solana's pool platforms.
  • A validator is a computer that confirms the network's transactions. Stake is the SOL people entrust to it. A stake pool gathers many people's SOL and spreads it across validators.
  • An epoch is Solana's "day" for stake. It lasts about two days.
  • A launch curve sells a new token at a rising price until it raises the amount its launchpad set. Then the curve is complete, and the token graduates to a trading pool. Meteora's Dynamic Bonding Curve (DBC) is one of them.

Notes on each type

Price markets read Pyth, an oracle where exchanges and market makers publish signed prices that the program checks on-chain. They're "touch" markets: YES if the price reaches the target at any moment before the end. Every entry needs a fresh price, so nobody can enter after the price has already touched. On 4 October 2026, Pyth had 1,881 price feeds, 423 of them crypto.

Epoch markets (stake pool size and validator stake) are read when an epoch begins, and the window to read them lasts about two days.

Launch graduations read the curve's own account on Meteora's DBC. Only curves priced in SOL, of 85 SOL or more, with at least 10% still to raise when the question opens. The target is the curve's own threshold, nothing else. Entries close the moment the curve completes. The answer goes by the second the curve's account recorded: completed by the end date is YES, even when it is read later. YES can be bought: anyone who pays the rest of the curve completes it at once, and the question's page says so. This type opens on devnet first, and on mainnet once the audits cover its reader.

Projects can push their own results, and that's the point. A project that promises a burn controls whether it happens. That's what it's putting money behind. Markets where the opener may control the result carry a warning: "The person who opened this market may control the result and may have picked a side."

What stays out, and why

Anything a single key decides for free. A key is the permission that gives someone power over a token or a program: to mint new tokens, freeze wallets or change code. Giving that power up forever is called renouncing. A market on a renunciation would let the key holder enter early from another wallet, flip the switch and collect, at no risk. So renunciations of minting or freezing, immutable programs and immutable metadata are all out.

Small tokens' prices, for now. In a small pool, the price is just the ratio of two reserves, and someone can move it for an instant with money borrowed and repaid in the same transaction. It has happened on Solana: in the Mango case, in October 2022, between $114 and $116 million was taken (SEC and CFTC complaints). Price markets start with assets that have an oracle price. Tokens with a pool on Raydium or Meteora come later, through the average price over a period of time.

Token-2022 and locked tokens, at first. Solana has two token programs, the classic one and the newer Token-2022. Burn markets start with classic tokens only. Newer tokens, which include recent pump.fun launches, come later, with a reader reviewed on its own.

Opinion, by design. Politics, geopolitics and anything that needs someone to say what happened. There are no opinion markets on Brot.

Sports, in phase 2. A match result isn't born on-chain: someone has to publish it. We'd rather build an oracle for it than borrow someone's word.

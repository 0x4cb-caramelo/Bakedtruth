Why the chain decides

In one bite: every prediction market needs a witness. We picked the one that can't be talked into anything.

Every prediction market has to answer one question at the end: what happened? Someone, or something, has to say it, and that's where markets break.

PlatformWho says what happened
PolymarketHolders of a token vote when a result is disputed. Disputes take 4 to 6 days
World, inside PhantomOne company key writes the result
PNPTwo language models decide

In March 2025, a Polymarket market worth $7 million settled as YES on an event that never happened.

Sources: Polymarket documentation; Chainstack Labs on World; PNP documentation; CoinDesk and Cointelegraph, March 2025.

Our answer: read it, don't ask it

Every Brot market points at a Solana account from the moment it opens: a token's supply, a pool's locked liquidity, a signed price. At the end, the program opens that account, reads the number and compares it with the target. If the target was met, the result is YES. If the end date passed without it, the result is NO. Nobody reports anything.

The result comes from the data, not from whoever triggered the reading. Anyone can open the same account and see the same number. How a market is decided walks through it step by step.

What it costs us

We can only list what the chain records. No elections, no sports, no "will this person tweet". The questions we can't list are exactly the ones where someone has to be trusted, so it's a trade we're happy with.

Where people still show up

We'd rather say it than have you find it. After the program reads a result, our team has a 6-hour window in which it can only confirm the result or annul the market, which refunds everyone. It can never switch YES and NO. The team can also pause the program, and a pause never blocks a payout or a refund.

No human picks the winner. Humans can only stop a wrong result from being paid.

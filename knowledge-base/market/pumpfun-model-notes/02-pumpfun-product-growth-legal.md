# pump.fun: product mechanics, growth playbook, abuse and legal exposure, and lessons for a launchpad of on-chain markets

Research date: 2026-10-07. Scope: January 2024 to October 2026.

Labels: **FACT** means a primary source (pump.fun's own docs or posts, a court filing, a regulator, a company press release, the original research report). **SECONDARY FACT** means press, an analyst or an aggregator reporting it. **INFERENCE** is this researcher's reasoning and is never stated as fact. "Not found" means I could not verify it.

Method note: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through this research. Some threads were closed with fetches of URLs already found, or with public data APIs (CourtListener, DefiLlama). Those threads are listed under Gaps. The 79-page opinion of 2026-08-31 in *Aguilar v. Baton Corp.* was downloaded from CourtListener's RECAP archive and read directly. It is the most important primary source in these notes.

Terminology: when these notes talk about Brot Markets they say markets, positions and participants. Third parties' own words ("prediction market", "wager", "bet") appear only inside quotes or as their product descriptions.

## 1. Which mechanics removed friction, and when? (product timeline, 2024 to Oct 2026)

### Takeaway
pump.fun made launching a token cost nothing and take seconds, with no code and no liquidity provider. A bonding-curve contract acts as the counterparty to every trade from the first second. After that, the product changes went in waves. Through 2024 it cut the cost of creating a token. In 2025 it built its own venue and paid creators. In 2026 it reworked fees and added non-memecoin products: bounties, tokenized-stock pairs and USDC pairs. Most of these waves answered a competitor or a fall in revenue.

### Cited Findings
**Core mechanics**
- FACT: anyone can create a coin, and trading is open to everyone from the start. The FAQ says: "Pump lets anyone create coins, giving everyone equal access to buy and sell from the start." [pump.fun FAQ](https://pump.fun/docs/faq), accessed 2026-10-07.
- FACT (the court's summary of the pleaded mechanics): "A purchaser sent SOL to a Pump.fun smart contract and received tokens; a seller sent tokens to the contract and received SOL... [Pump.fun] charged a one-percent fee on each." The contract itself is the counterparty, so no LP is needed at creation. [Aguilar v. Baton Corp., Dkt. 184, Opinion and Order, S.D.N.Y., 2026-08-31, pp. 51-52](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69593359/184/aguilar-v-baton-corporation-ltd-dba-pumpfun/)
- SECONDARY FACT (academic preprint, not peer reviewed): a token graduates at about 85 SOL of real deposits, or 115 SOL including 30 virtual SOL, which is roughly a $69,000 market cap. In a September 2025 sample, the median graduating token got there in about 4.4 minutes and about 457 bonding-curve swaps. [Marino, Naviglio, Tarantelli, Lillo, "Predicting the success of new crypto-tokens: the Pump.fun case", arXiv 2602.14860, 2026-02-16](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1)

**Cost to create, over time**
- SECONDARY FACT: at launch on 2024-01-19 (founders Noah Tweedale, Alon Cohen, Dylan Kerler), creating a token cost 0.02 SOL (about $3). [Wikipedia, citing the launch post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pump.fun); [Decrypt, Aug 2024](https://decrypt.co/243905/now-pump-fun-will-pay-you-to-create-a-successful-meme-coin?amp=1)
- SECONDARY FACT: on 2024-08-09 pump.fun moved the 0.02 SOL deployment fee to the coin's first buyer. It also began paying the creator 0.5 SOL when the bonding curve completes, funded out of a 1.5 SOL migration fee. Its reason: "This unlocks the use case of someone creating a coin without buying any of its supply." [The Block, 2024-08-09](https://www.theblock.co/post/310376/pump-fun-cuts-fees-and-unveils-80-incentive)
- FACT: current schedule (page last updated 2026-05-20). Coin creation "0 SOL / 0 USDC". Graduation to PumpSwap 0.015 SOL. Bonding-curve trades 1.25% in total: 0.30% to the creator, 0.95% to the protocol, 0% to LPs. Canonical PumpSwap pools are tiered by market cap, from 1.25% down to 0.30% in total. The page also lists USDC-quoted tokens, so a USDC base pair exists. [pump.fun Fees doc](https://pump.fun/docs/fees)

**Graduation and migration**
- SECONDARY FACT: in March 2025 pump.fun launched its own DEX, PumpSwap. Graduated tokens now migrate there instantly and free, instead of to Raydium, which removed "what was previously a 6 SOL fee". PumpSwap's launch fee was 0.25% (0.20% to LPs, 0.05% to the protocol). [The Block, Mar 2025](https://www.theblock.co/post/347360/pump-fun-launches-dex-called-pumpswap-to-instantly-migrate-graduated-tokens)
- CONFLICT: The Block's August 2024 piece speaks of a 1.5 SOL migration fee, and its March 2025 piece speaks of a 6 SOL fee being removed. I could not settle whether the fee changed between those dates or one report is wrong (see Gaps).

**Social and attention features**
- SECONDARY FACT: livestreaming existed in 2024 and was suspended on 2024-11-25 (details in section 4). [Decrypt, Nov 2024](https://decrypt.co/293495/pump-fun-drops-livestreaming-controversy)
- SECONDARY FACT: on 2025-04-04 livestreaming reopened to 5% of users under a moderation policy. pump.fun admitted that "some content — perhaps much content — generally defined as NSFW will in fact appear on Pump.fun." [The Block, 2025-04-04](https://www.theblock.co/post/349647/memecoin-launchpad-pump-fun-reopens-livestreaming-to-5-of-users-with-stricter-moderation-rules). It was later fully restored. [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/pumpfun-fully-restores-streaming-feature-with-stricter-moderation-policy/)

Enforcement is "termination of livestreams and user account(s)". Recordings are kept up to 30 days, and the policy admits "we may not discover every violation." [pump.fun Livestream moderation policy](https://pump.fun/docs/livestream-moderation-policy), accessed 2026-10-07

**Mobile app**

**Creator economics**
- SECONDARY FACT: "Project Ascend", a dynamic creator fee, arrived in early September 2025. It pays 0.95% per trade for tokens between $88k and $300k market cap and tapers to 0.05% at $20M. Creators received about $2M in the first 24 hours, against $198k the day before. [Decrypt, 2025-09-03](https://decrypt.co/337872/pump-funs-new-fee-model-hands-out-2m-to-creators-in-first-24-hours)

On 2026-03-25 each token got a single chance to redirect its creator fee, to stop "griefing". [Cointelegraph, 2026-03-25](https://cointelegraph.com/news/pump-fun-creator-fee-cap-update-memecoin-model)
- SECONDARY FACT (low-quality calendar aggregator): on 2026-09-12 "Holder Rewards" replaced Cashback mode. Trading fees are paid pro rata to holders several times per hour. [TradingView/CoinMarketCal, 2026-09-12](https://www.tradingview.com/news/coinmarketcal:df0f05658094b:0-pump-fun-holder-rewards-launch-replaces-cashback-mode-12-sep-2026/)

**Ecosystem funds, token, acquisitions and new products**
- SECONDARY FACT: on 2025-07-12 the PUMP token ICO raised about $500M in about 12 minutes, at $0.004 a token and a $4B fully diluted valuation. [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/markets/pump-fun-raises-500-million-minutes-pump-token-sale-fc226b29); [The Block](https://theblock.co/post/362320/pump-fun-raises-500-million-in-12-minutes-as-pump-tokens-fully-sell-out). [Decrypt, 2026-01-20](https://decrypt.co/355100/pump-fun-3-million-token-startup-fund)

They resolve in seconds to 30 minutes, "using direct official API calls or deterministic data". - SECONDARY FACT: on 2026-06-04 pump.fun launched **GO**, an escrowed marketplace of bounties paid in SOL that pump.fun approves. Abuse followed at once (section 4). [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/pumpfun-go-bounty-platform-backlash/); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/nfts-and-web3/pump-fun-launches-go-bounty-platform-backlash-extreme-listings)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: the friction cuts that mattered most came early and were structural: zero cost, zero code, zero LP and seconds to live. Later changes mostly redistributed fees (creator, trader, holder) to win back share or volume, which suggests that fee splits are a lever against competitors and not the source of the adoption.
- INFERENCE: pump.fun has moved toward the same territory as Brot. It owns the venue (PumpSwap), the terminal (Padre) and a USDC pair, and it invests in on-chain market startups (Pumpcade, and PumpMarket through its hackathon). It is a likely future competitor or acquirer in the on-chain-markets niche, not just an analogy.
- INFERENCE for Brot: the matching set of zeros would be: free market creation (or creation paid by the first participant, as pump.fun did in Aug 2024), no LP, a creation box that only accepts questions the program can verify, and live within seconds. The August 2024 idea of letting someone create without taking a position maps directly onto letting someone create a market without taking a side.

### Gaps
- Not found: the date livestreaming first launched in 2024.
- Not settled: the migration-fee history (1.5 SOL vs 6 SOL) and the exact PumpSwap launch day in March 2025.
- Not verified: the August 2026 "USDC integration" and "social callouts" dates. These come only from an AI-generated CoinMarketCap page, though the pump.fun Fees doc confirms USDC-quoted tokens exist. Also not verified: the Kolscan acquisition, and whether the 0.5 SOL creator reward still exists.

## 2. Growth playbook: how did pump.fun acquire users, and what metrics show the loop working?

### Takeaway
Growth came from a loop. Free launches produced thousands of new tokens a day. Each creator and early buyer had a financial reason to promote, because early entry on the curve is cheaper. Trading terminals, Telegram bots and KOLs on X carried that promotion to traders. Revenue peaked at about $150M a month in January 2025 and fell about 75% within a year, but pump.fun's share of Solana DEX volume kept rising after it launched PumpSwap. The court record shows that part of the "organic" promotion is alleged to have been coordinated by insiders.

### Cited Findings
**Revenue and volume (data, aggregated by this researcher)**
- SECONDARY FACT (DefiLlama revenue API, summed by month by this researcher on 2026-10-07): Mar 2024 $2.5M; Jul 2024 $28.6M; Nov 2024 $93.9M; **Jan 2025 $149.8M (peak)**; Jul 2025 $17.3M (the LetsBonk month); Sep 2025 $42.8M; Jan 2026 $31.9M; Aug 2026 $36.7M; Sep 2026 $33.1M. All-time revenue was about **$1.153B** at 2026-10-07. [DefiLlama API](https://api.llama.fi/summary/fees/pump.fun?dataType=dailyRevenue); [DefiLlama pump.fun](https://defillama.com/protocol/pump.fun)
- SECONDARY FACT: Cointelegraph's figures agree. January 2026 fees were $31.8M, down 75% from $148M in January 2025, and trading volume was down about 81% year on year. [Cointelegraph, 2026-03-25](https://cointelegraph.com/news/pump-fun-creator-fee-cap-update-memecoin-model)
- SECONDARY FACT (DefiLlama Solana DEX volume, my computation as pump.fun plus PumpSwap over all Solana DEXes): about 4-7% a month from May to December 2024; 12-18% from April to June 2025, after PumpSwap; about 9% in July 2025; 14% in August-September 2025; 21-35% from January to September 2026 (34.0% in March, 35.1% in July). Caveat: before PumpSwap, graduated pump.fun tokens traded on Raydium and count there, so the 2024 shares understate pump.fun's real influence. [DefiLlama Solana DEX API](https://api.llama.fi/overview/dexs/solana)

**Number of tokens**
- SECONDARY FACT: more than 1 million tokens by July 2024 (NYT, 2024-07-27) and more than 6 million by January 2025 (Wired), both cited by [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pump.fun). An average of more than 50,000 new coins a day in late November 2024. [Decrypt, Nov 2024](https://decrypt.co/293495/pump-fun-drops-livestreaming-controversy)
- SECONDARY FACT: from 2025-09-01 to 2025-10-01 there were 655,770 tokens created by 243,123 unique creators, of which 4,338 graduated, about **0.63%**. [arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1)
- FACT: more than 7 million tokens with at least five trades between January 2024 and March 2025. [Solidus Labs, May 2025](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance)

**Distribution channels**
- SECONDARY FACT (analyst newsletter): among Solana trading terminals and bots, Axiom's share of volume rose from 4.41% in February 2025 to 57.33% in July 2025, while Photon fell from 30.7% in January to under 10% in July. Axiom reached $200M of revenue in 202 days (by 2025-08-04). [Stepdata, 2025-08-08](https://stepdata.substack.com/p/solana-trading-bot-axiom-becomes)
- SECONDARY FACT: pump.fun justified buying Padre by saying trading terminals drive volume. [Cointelegraph, Oct 2025](https://cointelegraph.com/news/pumpfun-acquires-padre-trading-terminal-solana-memecoins)
- SECONDARY FACT: in August 2025 pump.fun's own X account began openly promoting tokens made on the platform (memes, commissioned art, reposts). Decrypt counts this among the reasons it won back the lead. [Decrypt, 2025-08-13](https://decrypt.co/335006/pump-fun-back-on-top-solana-meme-coins-soaring)
- SECONDARY FACT: to grow streaming, pump.fun funded content houses ("Basedd House"), sent streamers equipment kits, hired clippers and signed recruiter contracts. It brought in mainstream streamers and influencers (BunnyFuFuu, B Dave, Minikon). [Decrypt, 2025-12-29](https://decrypt.co/352919/creator-capital-markets-pump-fun-streaming-twitch)
- SECONDARY FACT: after Project Ascend in September 2025, pump.fun paid out more than $4M of creator fees in a single day. BunnyFuFuu earned $130k+ in his first three days, or $217k in two days by another count. [Decrypt, 2025-12-29](https://decrypt.co/352919/creator-capital-markets-pump-fun-streaming-twitch); [Decrypt search snippet](https://decrypt.co/337872/pump-funs-new-fee-model-hands-out-2m-to-creators-in-first-24-hours)

**The KOL loop as the plaintiffs allege it (not proven)**
- FACT that it is *alleged* (the court's summary of the complaint): insiders "pre-positioned" before promotion, spreading buys across wallets or "bundling" them at launch. pump.fun allegedly "recruited KOLs to publicize selected tokens and, in some instances, gave them compensation or insider information that was not disclosed", with some agreements requiring promotion to "appear 'organic'". Exits were allegedly staggered into retail demand ("exit liquidity"). These are pleaded allegations, not findings. [Dkt. 184, pp. 7-8](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69593359/184/aguilar-v-baton-corporation-ltd-dba-pumpfun/)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: the viral engine was that **every creator and early buyer is a marketer with a financial stake**. Prices on the curve rise with each buy, so promoting after you buy pays you directly. Terminals and bots turned thousands of daily launches into one tradable feed, and KOLs on X turned single tokens into events.
- INFERENCE: the metrics show the loop was strong but tied to the cycle. Revenue fell about 88% from January to July 2025 and about 75% year on year by January 2026. Share of Solana DEX volume still went up, because pump.fun took in more of the stack (venue, terminal) while the category shrank.
- INFERENCE for Brot: a pot-based market has a weaker built-in reason to promote. Everyone on the winning side gets the same payout per USDC whenever they joined, so a creator gains nothing from bringing in participants unless Brot pays the creator a share of the pot or fee, or weights early positions. Without one of those, the "creator promotes their own market" loop that drove pump.fun is mostly missing.
- INFERENCE for Brot: distribution through terminals matters. Brot's markets should be easy for third-party terminals and bots to index and enter (a clean IDL, events, deterministic accounts), because that is where Solana's active traders already are.

### Gaps
- Not found with good sourcing, because the search budget ran out: how pump.fun got its first users in January to March 2024 (the Solana memecoin-presale season, BOME/SLERF, and the "fair launch" framing as an answer to presale rugs). A low-quality blog claims 75,000+ tokens by March 2024 ([GetBlock](https://getblock.io/blog/what-is-pump-fun-and-how-to-benefit-from-it/)), which I did not verify.
- Not verified: a claim that about 70 of the top 100 Solana memecoins got backing from KOLs with 100k+ followers within 24 hours, and that bots make 60-80% of volume on some tokens. These appeared only in Forklog search snippets ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/?p=24119)).
- Not found: the share of pump.fun volume routed through terminals versus pump.fun's own front end and app; the 42,000-tokens-in-a-day record date ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/pumpfun-launched-42000-tokens-in-one-day-fewer-than-2-will-ever-reach-a-dex)); and Dune's tokens-per-day series (needs an API key).

## 3. Does the bonding-curve cold-start argument carry over to pot-based markets?

### Takeaway
Builders do make the argument that a pot needs no LP, the same property the bonding curve gave tokens. Pumpcade says it outright ("Parimutuel pools always work"), and PumpMarket and Melee build on it. The same builders also name its two weak points: no reason to enter early, and markets with one side only. The analogy holds for opening a market. It does not hold for whether every participant gets a counterparty.

### Cited Findings
- FACT (Pumpcade docs, primary): "Traditional order books need matched buyers and sellers. Parimutuel pools always work." Pumpcade adds time-weighted shares for an "early bird advantage" and says winners always get at least their deposit back. It takes 1% upfront: 0.45% to the protocol, 0.10% to the market creator, 0.45% to the streamer. In beta, "markets do not require predictions on both YES and NO sides", but production will need "1 participant on each side for a market to execute." [Pumpcade docs, Parimutuel Markets](https://docs.cade.market/mechanics/parimutuel-markets), accessed 2026-10-07
- FACT (company press release, self-reported): PumpMarket runs pot-based markets in SOL on whether a new pump.fun token will graduate within one hour, "settlement anchored to objective on-chain state changes". In its first 48 hours on mainnet: 63 markets resolved, 66 SOL of volume, 150+ positions. It entered pump.fun's Build in Public hackathon. [GlobeNewswire, 2026-02-26](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/02/26/3245639/0/en/pumpmarket-brings-prediction-markets-to-pump-fun-public-hackathon.html); [Crypto Briefing, 2026-02-18](https://cryptobriefing.com/crypto-prediction-market-launch/) (which says resolution uses Bitquery and Helius data, i.e. off-chain indexers reading on-chain state)
- SECONDARY FACT: Melee argues that classic pots dilute "early participants' returns... by late-stage capital", which discourages entering early. Its "Parimutuel Market Maker" lets a market start "from the first capital contribution" with no professional market maker, adding dynamic pricing, a minimum-return floor and an LP vault for cash-outs. [AICoin, 2026-07-22](https://www.aicoin.com/en/news-flash/2996333); [Solana Compass, Melee](https://solanacompass.com/projects/melee-markets)
- SECONDARY FACT: on pump.fun's side of the analogy, the fastest early inflow of liquidity is "the strongest predictor of graduation", and only about 0.63% of tokens graduated in September 2025. [arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: the bonding curve solved **two** cold-start problems at once. (a) Liquidity: there is always a price and someone to trade with, namely the contract. (b) Incentive: the first buyer pays the least, which rewards early entry and promotion. A plain pot solves (a) only in a weaker form. A market can open with no LP, but a YES participant has nothing to win until someone takes NO. It does not solve (b) at all, because the pot pays every winning USDC the same.
- INFERENCE: the "empty pot" problem is the counterpart of pump.fun's 99%+ of tokens that never graduate. It is harsher, though. A pump.fun buyer in a dead token can still sell back to the curve at a loss. A Brot participant in a one-sided market just gets a refund, so the experience is "nothing happened". If Brot copies pump.fun's free creation, most markets will probably be one-sided or tiny. The product needs a defined outcome for those (an automatic refund with no fee) and a feed that surfaces markets with two live sides.
- INFERENCE: ways to bring back the early-entry incentive within a pot are time-weighted shares (Pumpcade), a creator share of the fee (Pumpcade's 0.10%, pump.fun's creator fees) and a seed from the creator on the side they doubt. Each one adds complexity or a new way to game the system, and that trade-off should be decided deliberately and written down.
- INFERENCE for Brot's creation box: PumpMarket shows that markets on pump.fun's own on-chain events (graduation within N minutes) are a natural, high-frequency category that can be resolved from on-chain data. pump.fun itself has funded a neighbour in this space.

### Gaps
- Not found: an independent essay or analyst thread (not by a builder) that argues the bonding-curve and pot cold-start analogy either way, or that measures how often pot-based markets stay one-sided. The search budget ran out before I could look on X or in research newsletters.
- Not verified: Pumpcade's mainnet status and volumes after Q2 2026, and PumpMarket's traction after its first 48 hours.

## 4. Abuse and harm: what went wrong, and how did pump.fun respond each time?

### Takeaway
Harm came from three places. (1) Token economics: almost every token collapses, dumps by the creator or insiders are common, and snipers and bundlers take the first block. (2) Features that pay for attention: livestreams in 2024 and GO bounties in 2026 produced threats of self-harm and violence. (3) Insider and security failures: the May 2024 exploit by a former employee. pump.fun's pattern was to pause the feature, write a moderation policy and relaunch it to a small share of users. It made fee-rule changes against griefing, and it did not build anything against sniping at protocol level.

### Cited Findings
**How many tokens fail or are dumped**
- FACT (the original report, read through a fetch summary): Solidus Labs' "2025 Rug Pull Report" (May 2025; Forklog dates it 2025-05-08) covers more than 7 million pump.fun tokens with at least five trades from January 2024 to March 2025. Only about 97,000 still held at least $1,000 of liquidity, which is ~1.4%, so **98.6% had fallen below $1,000** and were "essentially worthless". The report also refers to 98.7% showing "characteristics of pump-and-dump schemes". It found that 93% of 388,000 Raydium pools showed "soft rug pull" traits, with a median of about $2,832 and a maximum of $1.9M. Caveat: the liquidity test does not tell natural failure apart from deliberate fraud. [Solidus Labs](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance); [Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/report-98-of-pump-funs-memecoins-deemed-scams/)
- CONFLICT and wording: the press (BeInCrypto, Bitcoin.com, The Defiant: "99%") reported it as "98.6% are rug pulls or pump-and-dumps", but the measurement is collapse of liquidity. The 98.6% figure is also often confused with "share that never graduated", which was ~99.4% in September 2025 by the arXiv count. [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/pump-fun-tokens-scams-solidus-labs-report/); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/alarming-99-of-memecoin-launches-on-pumpfun-are-pump-and-dumps-or-rug-pulls-report)
- SECONDARY FACT: of 184,282 tokens studied, 92.22% showed at least one dump event. Creators have a "clear incentive to dump while the token is still on the bonding curve". Tokens with a lot of bot-like activity graduate less often. [arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860v1)

**Bots, snipers and bundlers**

**Platform rules**
- FACT: the Terms let pump.fun "remove, modify or reject any content", warn users, and remove "User Materials and User-Generated Digital Assets" at its "sole discretion". [pump.fun Terms, updated 2026-09-25, §20.2](https://pump.fun/docs/terms-and-conditions)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: every feature that **pays for attention** (livestreams, bounties) brought out the worst behaviour within hours or days, and pump.fun only moderated after the fact. Brot's rule that markets resolve from on-chain accounts only is a structural shield here. Questions like "will streamer X do Y" cannot be created, so this class of harm cannot happen on Brot.
- INFERENCE: the abuse that *does* carry over to on-chain markets is **creator influence over the outcome**. A creator who controls the account a market reads (a wallet balance, a token supply, a program's state) can pick a side and then make it happen. New York's definition of gambling itself requires an event "not under his control or influence" (section 5). The creation box and the reader set should reject any question whose target account the creator, or anyone, can cheaply move. That is the same reasoning the frozen pred-provable program already uses against anything a flash loan can move.
- INFERENCE: sniping and bundling map onto **last-second positions with information others lack** in pots. The anti-sniping cutoff in Brot's lineage (`require_trading_open`) corresponds to what pump.fun never built.
- INFERENCE: the May 2024 exploit came from an insider with **admin privileges** on contracts holding user funds. For Brot, any admin or upgrade key over the pots is the matching single point of failure. Limiting withdraw-type authorities, and having a pause plus restitution plan ready, is the lesson pump.fun learned at a cost of $1.9M.

### Gaps
- Not found: whether pump.fun ever added protocol-level anti-sniping or anti-bundling to the bonding curve; what moderation GO had later, and whether the suicide-linked listing was removed; and how many streams or accounts were banned after the 2025 relaunch.
- Not verified: the lending protocol used in the May 2024 flash loans (some reports name one; I did not confirm it).

## 5. Legal and regulatory exposure: what happened, and which risks transfer to a platform where the public creates event markets?

### Takeaway
The US class action narrowed sharply on 2026-08-31. Solana Labs, the Solana Foundation and their executives were dismissed. The securities claims and **all gambling predicates** failed. RICO claims survive against Baton Corp and its three founders on two predicates: wire fraud, based on the "unruggable / fair launch / even playing field" posts, and **unlicensed money transmission**. The ruling's reasons point the opposite way for Brot. The court cleared pump.fun of gambling *because* a token purchase is not a payout that depends on a future event, and it expressly reserved the question for contracts that are. A pot-based market is that kind of contract.

### Cited Findings
**The US case: *Aguilar v. Baton Corporation Ltd. d/b/a Pump.Fun*, No. 1:25-cv-00880-CM (S.D.N.Y.)**
- FACT: filed on 2025-01-30, before Judge Colleen McMahon. Parties include Baton Corp, Cohen, Kerler, Tweedale, Solana Labs, the Solana Foundation, Yakovenko, Gokal, Albert, Federa, Liu, Jito Labs, the Jito Foundation, Lucas Bruder, Brian Smith, Aaron Kvenild and "Lead KOL Does 1-25". The plaintiffs are Aguilar, Carnahan and Okafor. Firms include Burwick Law and Wolf Popper. [CourtListener API record of the docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69593359/aguilar-v-baton-corporation-ltd-dba-pumpfun/)
- SECONDARY FACT: on 2025-07-23 an amended complaint added RICO claims against the Solana and Jito entities and executives. It described pump.fun as "merely the front-facing slot machine cabinet" of an "illegal gambling and money transmission scheme" and alleged $4-5.5B of retail losses. [The Block, 2025-07-23](https://www.theblock.co/post/363996/burwick-law-names-solana-and-jito-executives-in-expanded-rico-case-against-pump); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/331528/solana-pump-fun-amended-rico-suit-alleging-meme-coin-gambling)
- SECONDARY FACT: in December 2025 (ruling of 2025-12-09) the judge allowed a Second Amended Complaint, which drew on about 5,000 internal chat logs from a confidential informant about transaction ordering, validator behaviour and priority execution. [Yellow, 2025-12-16](https://yellow.com/news/judge-approves-expansion-of-solana-pumpfun-lawsuit-after-5000-internal-chats-surface); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/mev-trading-returns-court-pump-fun-class-action-lawsuit)
- FACT: the Second Amended Complaint was filed on 2026-01-08 per the court (Burwick Law says 2026-01-07). [Dkt. 184](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69593359/184/aguilar-v-baton-corporation-ltd-dba-pumpfun/); [Burwick Law case page](https://www.burwick.law/active-cases/pump-fun-and-solana-rico-lawsuit-aguilar-v-baton-corporation)
- SECONDARY FACT: Burwick voluntarily dropped Jito Labs months before the ruling. [Protos, 2026-09-01](https://protos.com/solana-labs-dropped-from-burwick-laws-pump-fun-lawsuit/); [Crypto Economy](https://crypto-economy.com/judge-dismisses-burwick-law-claims-and-drops-solana-labs-from-pump-fun-case/). The motions decided on 2026-08-31 involve no Jito party, which fits.

**The 2026-08-31 Opinion and Order (Dkt. 184, 79 pp.), the primary source**
- FACT: "Counts I and II, for substantive RICO and RICO conspiracy... remain pending only as asserted by Kendall Carnahan and Michael Okafor against Baton Corporation Ltd., Alon Cohen, Dylan Kerler, and Noah Tweedale." All of Aguilar's claims, which failed the domestic-injury test, and "all claims asserted against Solana Labs, Inc., the Solana Foundation, Anatoly Yakovenko, Raj Gokal, Dan Albert, Austin Federa, and Lily Liu, are DISMISSED." The Securities Act counts (§§5, 12(a)(1), 15) were dismissed as to FRED and GRIFFAIN on the merits and as to the other 18 tokens for lack of class standing. Unjust enrichment was dismissed. Dismissals under 12(b)(6) are with prejudice. Plaintiffs had to show cause by 2026-09-10 why the unserved KOL Does should not be dropped. [Dkt. 184, docket text](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69593359/184/aguilar-v-baton-corporation-ltd-dba-pumpfun/)
- FACT, wire fraud from marketing: the actionable statements were Cohen's of 2024-09-25 (pump.fun is an "unruggable fair launch platform"), 2024-10-04 ("pump fun is as much of an even playing field as it gets IMO") and 2024-10-12 ("the playing field is as fair as ever"). The court held these "did not merely express Cohen's subjective enthusiasm" but "communicated something factual... that there were no favored participants who might have some undisclosed advance access." [Dkt. 184, pp. 39-40](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69593359/184/aguilar-v-baton-corporation-ltd-dba-pumpfun/)
- FACT, gambling rejected: "Plaintiffs' asserted gambling predicates fail as against all moving Defendants." Under NY Penal Law §225.00(2), gambling is staking value "upon the outcome of a contest of chance or a future contingent event not under his control or influence, upon an agreement or understanding that he will receive something of value in the event of a certain outcome." A pump.fun buyer "received the token immediately... The amount received was the consideration for his relinquishing ownership of the token. It was not a prize or a payout that became due because some specified contingent event occurred." And: "the purchase of some asset does not become a wager merely because its resale value depends on uncertain future demand." [Dkt. 184, pp. 45-47](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69593359/184/aguilar-v-baton-corporation-ltd-dba-pumpfun/)
- FACT, the test the court quoted (from *Liss v. Manuel*, 1968): "If a sum of money is put up merely to abide a contingent event, at which time the event will determine to whom the money shall go, the transaction is clearly a wager." [Dkt. 184, p. 46](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69593359/184/aguilar-v-baton-corporation-ltd-dba-pumpfun/)
- FACT, **footnote 6, the question it reserved**: "The Court does not address a contract under which the parties' rights to payment are themselves determined by the occurrence or nonoccurrence of an identified future event. Whether such a contract constitutes gambling under § 225.00(2) presents an entirely different question, one that is not presently before the Court." [Dkt. 184, p. 47 n.6](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69593359/184/aguilar-v-baton-corporation-ltd-dba-pumpfun/)
- FACT, bookmaking: "'Bookmaking' means 'advancing gambling activity by unlawfully accepting bets from members of the public as a business . . . upon the outcomes of future contingent events.'" It failed against pump.fun only because buying a token is not a bet. [Dkt. 184, p. 49](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69593359/184/aguilar-v-baton-corporation-ltd-dba-pumpfun/)
- FACT, **unlicensed money transmission survived** (18 U.S.C. §1960): "Pump.fun's smart contracts likewise allegedly accepted one form of value and transmitted another as part of an exchange operated for a fee", with no FinCEN registration alleged. That was plausible under *United States v. Goklu* (2d Cir. 2026). The founders were treated as active "intermediaries". By contrast, Solana Labs and the Foundation fell within FinCEN's exclusion for those who only provide "delivery, communication, or network access services", and a software developer "is not a money transmitter for the mere act of creating the application." [Dkt. 184, pp. 50-53](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69593359/184/aguilar-v-baton-corporation-ltd-dba-pumpfun/)
- FACT, damages: the trading-loss theory failed RICO's directness test. Execution losses ("inferior fills", slippage from favored bots) and the theory that users "would not have paid the one-percent transaction fees" passed. [Dkt. 184, pp. 55-62](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/69593359/184/aguilar-v-baton-corporation-ltd-dba-pumpfun/)
- CONFLICT: Protos, Crypto Economy and The Currency Analytics wrote that RICO claims "of wire fraud, illegal gambling, and unlicensed money transmission were upheld". The opinion itself says the gambling predicates **fail** against every defendant. [Protos](https://protos.com/solana-labs-dropped-from-burwick-laws-pump-fun-lawsuit/); [Crypto Times, 2026-09-01](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/09/01/judge-dismisses-pump-fun-securities-claims-clears-solana-labs-and-foundation/) (this one reports it correctly, though its summary misdates the ruling to 2025). The primary source wins.
- SECONDARY FACT: the Wolf Popper firm's summary: "the complaint plausibly alleges an 'association-in-fact' enterprise consisting of Baton, its founders, and 25 unidentified memecoin promoters." [Wolf Popper](https://www.wolfpopper.com/news/court-allows-rico-claims-against-pumpfun-and-founders-to-proceed)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE, the central point for Brot: the reasoning that cleared pump.fun of gambling **does not protect a pot-based market**. In Brot, participants put USDC into a pot "merely to abide a contingent event", and the outcome decides "to whom the money shall go". That is the *Liss* description of "clearly a wager" that the court quoted. The house fee from the losing side makes the operator look like someone "accepting bets from members of the public as a business" (bookmaking). Footnote 6 leaves exactly this case open. The plaintiffs' bar has already used the RICO gambling route (18 U.S.C. §1955 and NY Penal Law §225) against a Solana launchpad, so Brot should expect it to be tried again if US or New York participants can reach the product. This is a reason to settle jurisdiction, geo-blocking and the regulatory route (for example CFTC event contracts versus gambling licensing) before opening market creation to the public. It does not rule the model out, but it is the biggest risk that carries over.
- INFERENCE, money transmission carries over directly: a program that accepts USDC and pays it out to winners for a fee looks like what the court found plausible under §1960 ("accepted one form of value and transmitted another... for a fee"). The FinCEN exclusion that saved Solana Labs covers *infrastructure*, not the operator of the app that runs and profits from the flow.
- INFERENCE, marketing claims become fraud predicates: "unruggable", "fair launch" and "even playing field" were treated as factual statements. Brot's pitch around on-chain resolution ("can't be manipulated", "no oracle", "trustless") must be worded exactly ("resolved by reading account X at slot Y") and must be true. Otherwise it becomes the same kind of predicate.
- INFERENCE, insider access is the core of the fraud theory: the alleged harm was insiders getting early token addresses, priority execution and KOL coordination. The Brot equivalent would be the team, or favoured creators, knowing about markets before they appear in the feed, or entering positions with privileged ordering before cutoff. Publishing markets at the same moment for everyone, with no private access, is the design answer.
- INFERENCE, the Terms did not prevent litigation: despite a BVI governing-law clause, mandatory arbitration and a class-action waiver, the US class action reached a 79-page ruling on the merits of the pleadings. Whether Baton ever moved to compel arbitration is not known (see Gaps). "Platform, not issuer" language did not stop the operator and founders from being treated as RICO participants.
- INFERENCE, the founders are named personally: Cohen, Kerler and Tweedale are individual defendants on the surviving counts. For a small team this is a personal risk, not only a company risk.
- INFERENCE, app stores act as regulators: Apple's delisting followed a new financial-instrument feature within a day. A Brot mobile app would face the same review exposure.
- INFERENCE, the UK pattern: one FCA warning was enough for pump.fun to exit the UK for good. The UK is already on pump.fun's banned list, and a pot-based market is closer to regulated gambling or betting there than memecoins are.

### Gaps
- Not found: whether Baton moved to compel arbitration under its Terms, and with what outcome; what was filed after 2026-08-31 (the show-cause response due 2026-09-10, answers, any interlocutory appeal); the exact date Jito was dropped; any other US suits against pump.fun (for example the Burwick suit against Magic Eden mentioned in a summary); and pump.fun's reported $5M-salary search for a legal chief ([Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/pump-fun-legal-chief-court-drama-persists/), snippet only).
- Not found, because the search budget ran out: warnings or bans from regulators other than the FCA (EU, Asia, LatAm), and the reason for the India App Store delisting.
- Out of scope here and to be checked elsewhere: CFTC event-contract rules and US state gambling law as they apply to Brot's own model. The pump.fun opinion only shows the question is open, not how it would be decided.

## 6. Competition among launchpads: what decided share, and how durable was it?

### Takeaway
Leadership in launches swung in days rather than months. In two weeks of August 2025 pump.fun went from about 5% to about 90% of Solana launches, as bot deployers and fee incentives moved. Rivals won share with token-holder buybacks and ecosystem injections (LetsBonk), a new launch channel (Believe via replies on X; Zora inside the Base App), or a chain-level narrative (Four.meme in "BNB meme season"). Each lead lasted weeks. pump.fun's more durable assets were owning the venue (PumpSwap), its brand and cash, and later the terminal (Padre).

- INFERENCE: **launch-share leadership is not a moat.** Serial deployers, often bots, and terminal users can move between launchpads at almost no cost, because terminals list every launchpad side by side. - INFERENCE: what lasted was **owning more of the stack**: the canonical venue after graduation (PumpSwap's fee tiers), the terminal (Padre), the mobile app, the streaming culture, and an ICO war chest that paid for buybacks and injections. A launch box alone is easy to copy, as LetsBonk showed (its technical base was not verified here).
- INFERENCE for Brot: if Brot proves that free, public, verifiable market creation works, a copy is likely within weeks, and pump.fun itself could be one (it already funds Pumpcade, and PumpMarket entered its hackathon). Brot's defensible pieces would be what is hard to copy fast: a library of verified readers (each account layout checked against real bytes), resolution that has a track record of being correct, and the liquidity of its best markets. A creation form is not one of them.

## 7. Synthesis: why pump.fun worked, where it hurt people, and what Brot should copy or avoid

### Takeaway
pump.fun worked because it removed every upfront cost of creation (money, code, liquidity, waiting), gave every creator and early buyer a financial reason to promote, and plugged into the terminals and social channels where traders already were. It hurt people because the same zero-cost, attention-paid design rewarded insiders, bots and dumps, and its features that paid for attention invited self-harm stunts. Its legal exposure comes from fairness marketing that the plaintiffs say was false, and from acting as a fee-taking intermediary of value. Brot can copy the zero-friction creation and the legibility for terminals. It should avoid absolute fairness marketing, privileged access and features that pay for attention. It should treat gambling law as the main open risk, because a pot is exactly the contract type the pump.fun court left undecided.

### Inferences
**Copy**
- INFERENCE: free creation, or creation paid by the first participant (pump.fun, August 2024), in seconds and without code. Brot's creation box with autocomplete is the counterpart of pump.fun's no-code form, and it also acts as a safety filter, which pump.fun never had.
- INFERENCE: a feed that ranks live markets (the "king of the hill" idea), but ranked by markets with **two live sides** and volume, so that empty pots stay out of view.
- INFERENCE: a clearly defined reward for creators (a share of the house fee, as Pumpcade's 0.10% creator cut does) so that the creator has a reason to promote, without letting creators redirect fees after the fact (pump.fun's March 2026 fix).
- INFERENCE: legibility for terminals and bots (an IDL, events, deterministic PDAs), because terminals were pump.fun's distribution channel.

**Avoid**
- INFERENCE: absolute fairness or safety claims in marketing. Describe instead the exact account and slot read for each market.
- INFERENCE: any private or early access to markets for the team, partners or KOLs, and any priority entry before cutoff. Undisclosed paid promotion should be banned in the Terms and not practised by the team.
- INFERENCE: features that pay for attention (livestreams, bounties, markets on what a person will do). Brot's on-chain-only rule already excludes them, and it should stay that way.
- INFERENCE: questions whose target account the creator, or a flash loan, can move. This is both a manipulation risk and part of the legal definition of gambling ("not under his control or influence").
- INFERENCE: admin keys able to withdraw from pots (the May 2024 exploit). Keep a pause and restitution playbook ready anyway.

**Decide before opening market creation to the public**
- INFERENCE: jurisdiction and geo-blocking (the US and New York in particular, and the UK, given the FCA precedent), and the regulatory route for pot-based event markets. The pump.fun opinion shows the gambling question is open for event-contingent payouts. The §1960 theory shows that a fee-taking flow of value can be pleaded as unlicensed money transmission.

### Gaps
- These notes cannot say how a US court would classify Brot's pots. Footnote 6 reserves the question, and no ruling on that point was found. That needs specialist legal advice, and Brot's own regulation notes should be checked against these findings.
